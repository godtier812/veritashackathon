## Part 2 — Eleven Judge Questions and Answers

### 1. How do you know it works?
We froze every cutoff and model parameter on the RING start region (`1–101`: `131` `LOF` harmful, `385` `FUNC` harmless in `outputs/frozen.json`) and executed `src/evaluate.py` once (`run=1` in `outputs/results/run_log.txt`) on the held-out BRCT end region (`1631–1855`: `267` `LOF` harmful, `899` `FUNC` harmless). As recorded word for word in `outputs/results/result_sentences.txt`, `"A model that only read protein sequences ranked harmful BRCA1 mutations better than a classic substitution table."` and `"To catch 94.4% of harmful mutations, the model also flagged 437 of 899 harmless ones."` On that held-out end region (`outputs/results/headline.csv`, `outputs/results/auroc.csv`), zero-shot `ESM-2` (`arm2`, cutoff `3.034498`) caught `252` of `267` harmful mutations (`94.4%`, `15` missed) with `437` of `899` false alarms (`48.6%`) and `AUROC` `0.8995`, whereas the `BLOSUM62` rule (`arm1`, cutoff `0.0`) caught `232` of `267` (`86.9%`, `35` missed) with `571` of `899` false alarms (`63.5%`) and `AUROC` `0.6681`. Across `1,000` position-level bootstrap resamples of the `225` end-region positions (`outputs/results/bootstrap.json`), the `esm_minus_blosum` `AUROC` gain was `+0.231` (95% interval `+0.188` to `+0.272`), and Spearman correlation with lab `function_score` (`outputs/results/sanity7.json`) was `0.506` across all `1,837` rows (`0.430` on `575` start rows, `0.538` on `1,262` end rows).

### 2. The lab already tested these mutations. Who needs your tool?
BRCA1 is our exam, not the use case: Findlay et al. (2018) already measured `96.5%` of all possible single-letter DNA changes at these `326` positions (`1,837` single-letter protein swaps in `data/brca1_missense_scores_slim.csv`), so a genetic counselor checking one of these BRCA1 positions would use the lab result directly. Most human genes, however, have no saturation CRISPR lab map when a patient's test returns a "variant of uncertain significance" (VUS), leaving counselors to rely on computer predictions where no answer key exists. Grading zero-shot `ESM-2` against the BRCA1 answer key with pre-frozen cutoffs measures how far a sequence-only model can be trusted on an held-out protein domain (`252` of `267` harmful mutations caught, `94.4%`) and what trade-off it carries (`437` of `899` harmless mutations flagged, `48.6%`, and `15` harmful mutations missed).

### 3. Did the model see BRCA1 during training?
Yes, `ESM-2` (`facebook/esm2_t33_650M_UR50D`) read natural protein sequences from UniRef—which includes the normal 1,863-letter human BRCA1 wild-type sequence—during its self-supervised masked-language-model pretraining. However, `ESM-2` was trained only to predict masked amino acid letters from surrounding sequence letters and never saw patient records, disease phenotypes, or any Findlay et al. (2018) CRISPR lab `function_score` or `LOF`/`FUNC` label. In our pipeline, `arm2` computes zero-shot log-odds (`esm_score = log P(new_letter) - log P(wild_type)`) without modifying any `ESM-2` weights, using the `516` clear-class start-region rows (`131` `LOF`, `385` `FUNC`) only to set the frozen decision cutoffs (`cutoff_a = 3.034498`, `cutoff_b = 7.751908` in `outputs/frozen.json`).

### 4. Why not split the data at random?
The `1,837` lab-tested mutations sit across `326` positions (`101` in `start` `1–101` and `225` in `end` `1631–1855`, averaging `5.63` tested swaps per position), and mutations at the same position share the exact same wild-type letter, 500-letter window context, and position `entropy`. Splitting rows at random would put sibling swaps from the same amino acid site into both tuning and test sets—for example, at PDB `1JM7` zinc-holding position `61` (`outputs/heatmap.png`), all `6` lab-tested swaps (`C61F`, `C61G`, `C61R`, `C61S`, `C61W`, `C61Y`) are `LOF` and all `19` swaps have negative `esm_score` values (`-15.61` to `-6.02` across all `152` swaps at the `8` zinc-holding positions `24, 27, 39, 41, 44, 47, 61, 64`), so random splits would leak position-level answers. Instead, Rule 2 locked all tuning on the RING start region (`1–101`, with `arm3` cross-validation grouped by `position`) and tested once on the separate BRCT end region (`1631–1855`), and our bootstrap (`outputs/results/bootstrap.json`) likewise resampled the `225` end-region positions rather than individual rows.

### 5. Isn't this already on the ProteinGym leaderboard?
Yes—we state plainly in our write-up and notebook that ProteinGym already benchmarks `ESM-2` zero-shot on `BRCA1_HUMAN_Findlay_2018.csv` and that a public Evo 2 notebook scores DNA variants from the same 2018 study, so grading a language model on this dataset is not a new method. What ProteinGym reports is a single whole-table rank correlation (`Spearman`), which does not tell a counselor how a pre-frozen decision cutoff performs on an unseen domain or how many harmless variants get flagged alongside caught harmful ones. We added four specific pieces: holding out the entire BRCT end region (`1631–1855`) with cutoffs frozen in advance (`outputs/frozen.json`), testing whether start-region labels help at the other end (`arm3`), counting missed harmful cases and false alarms separately (`outputs/results/headline.csv`), and inspecting specific failure cases (`outputs/results/failures.md`). After the single test run finished, we also recorded the full-table Spearman correlation (`0.5056` across all `1,837` rows in `outputs/results/sanity7.json`) as a post-test sanity check. Also after the test run, we recorded where that sits on ProteinGym's published leaderboard for this table: `ESM-2` 650M is 16th of 97 zero-shot models by Spearman (published `0.515`, best `0.560`, median `0.456`; `outputs/results/reference_proteingym.json`), and the larger `ESM-2` models score lower there (`0.497` for 3B, `0.394` for 15B).

### 6. How is this different from AlphaMissense?
AlphaMissense is a specialized missense predictor that combines multiple-sequence alignments, AlphaFold structural context, and weak supervision from population frequency data, whereas our `arm2` (`facebook/esm2_t33_650M_UR50D`) uses only a single 500-letter wild-type protein sequence window (`A`: `1–500` or `B`: `1364–1863`) and masked-marginal token log-probabilities. As far as we know, precomputed AlphaMissense predictions are free to download and reach higher benchmark scores, so we never claim our prototype is better or cheaper than AlphaMissense. Our project uses a small, self-contained sequence-only model as an open exam so every cutoff (`outputs/frozen.json`), missed harmful mutation (`15` of `267`), false alarm (`437` of `899`), swap-kind breakdown (`outputs/results/by_swap.csv`), and failure case (`outputs/results/failures.md`) can be inspected directly. After the test run we scored AlphaMissense's published BRCA1 predictions as a reference row on the same `1,166` held-out rows with the same start-region cutoff rule (`outputs/results/reference_alphamissense.json`): AUROC `0.9155` vs `0.8995` for `arm2` (difference `+0.016`, 95% position-bootstrap interval `-0.003` to `+0.038`), and at its cutoff it caught `224` of `267` harmful mutations (`43` missed) with `124` of `899` false alarms, against `252` caught (`15` missed) with `437` false alarms for `arm2`. It is a reference from a different kind of tool, added after our one test run, not one of our arms.

### 7. Why 90%? What happens with a different cutoff?
When screening for loss-of-function BRCA1 mutations, missing a harmful variant is more costly than flagging a harmless one for follow-up, so Rule 4 locked the highest start-region harm score that caught at least `90%` (`118` of `131`) of start-region `LOF` mutations (`cutoff_a = 3.034498` for `arm2`, which caught `118` of `131` = `90.1%` with `196` of `385` = `50.9%` false alarms on `start`, and caught `252` of `267` = `94.4%` with `437` of `899` = `48.6%` false alarms on `end`). Raising the harm-score cutoff lowers false alarms but misses more harmful variants: in `outputs/frozen.json` and `outputs/results/band_counts.csv`, moving from `cutoff_a = 3.034498` (`esm_score <= -3.03`) to the start-region 10%-false-alarm threshold `cutoff_b = 7.751908` (`esm_score <= -7.75`, `"Looks harmful"`) reduces held-out end-region `FUNC` false alarms from `437` (`48.6%`) to `92` (`10.2%`), while `LOF` caught drops from `252` (`94.4%`) to `196` (`73.4%`), placing `56` `LOF` and `345` `FUNC` rows into the `"Can't tell"` band between the two cutoffs. On the held-out end region, `"Looks harmful"` (`harm_arm2 >= 7.751908`) matched the lab (`LOF`) on `196` of `288` clear-class calls (`68.1%`, plus `29` `INT`), while `"Looks harmless"` (`harm_arm2 < 3.034498`) matched the lab (`FUNC`) on `462` of `477` clear-class calls (`96.9%`, with `15` `LOF` misses and `20` `INT`).

### 8. What would make it fail?
First, because `ESM-2` reads only the 20-letter protein sequence, it is completely blind to DNA mutations that break RNA splicing before translation—a limit we could not test directly because `data/` has no supplementary splicing column (`outputs/results/failures.md`). Factually, in `outputs/results/failures.md`, 3 of Arm 2's 5 worst misses are at adjacent positions `1823` and `1824` (`A1823S` at rank 1 with `function_score = -3.642831` and `esm_score = +0.746405`, `A1823T` at rank 2 with `function_score = -3.186519` and `esm_score = +0.259899`, and `I1824M` at rank 3 with `function_score = -2.078020` and `esm_score = -0.979514`), where our explained wrong case `A1823S` was called `"Looks harmless"` and missed by all three arms (`flag_arm1 = 0`, `flag_arm2 = 0`, `flag_arm3 = 0`). Guess: High sequence entropy at positions `1823` (`2.572233`) and `1824` (`2.362860`) together with mild amino acid swaps (`BLOSUM62` `+1` or `0`) made these changes look benign to protein-sequence models, whereas the severe lab `LOF` scores may reflect DNA-level splicing disruption or a local assay requirement invisible to `ESM-2`. Second, `ESM-2` also raises false alarms on harmless variants (`437` of `899` `FUNC` rows flagged on `end`, plus `76` of `96` `INT` rows in `outputs/results/int_rows.csv`), especially on `loss_of_cysteine` (`15` of `20` = `75.0%` false alarms in `outputs/results/by_swap.csv`) and `charge_change` (`178` of `310` = `57.4%`), or when a tolerated position receives a proline swap such as rank-1 false alarm `L1844P` (`esm_score = -12.934240`, `FUNC` `function_score = -0.232682`; Guess: the `L`-to-`P` `to_proline` swap and `-3` `BLOSUM62` score triggered a steep penalty even though high-entropy position `1844` [`2.041959`] tolerated proline in the lab assay).

### 9. Would you use it on a patient?
No—we would never use this tool on a patient, to diagnose any condition, or to classify variants for clinical care: `"This project is a prototype and is not medical advice."` Even on the held-out BRCT end region where `arm2` caught `252` of `267` harmful mutations (`94.4%`), it still missed `15` harmful `LOF` mutations (`5.6%`), falsely flagged `437` of `899` harmless `FUNC` mutations (`48.6%`, `outputs/results/headline.csv`), and flagged `76` of `96` intermediate `INT` mutations (`79.2%`, `outputs/results/int_rows.csv`). In addition, the lab answer key measures cell survival in a dish rather than human patients (and a 2025 follow-up preprint notes cell-type sensitivity), our data are mutations rather than people (so we cannot check performance by age, sex, or ancestry), and the protein model is blind to DNA splicing.

### 11. Why did Arm 3 (logistic regression on ESM-2 + BLOSUM62 + chemistry) rank worse than zero-shot ESM-2 on the held-out end region?
On the held-out BRCT end region (`1631–1855`), `arm3` had a held-out `AUROC` of `0.8662` vs `arm2` `AUROC` `0.8995` (`outputs/results/auroc.csv`), and across `1,000` position-bootstrap resamples (`outputs/results/bootstrap.json`), the `arm3_minus_esm` 95% position-bootstrap interval was `-0.054` to `-0.014` (point `-0.033`, entirely below zero). At its frozen out-of-fold 90%-catch threshold (`cutoff = 0.099752`), `arm3` also had `22` vs `15` missed harmful mutations out of `267` (`245` vs `252` caught, `91.8%` vs `94.4%` catch rate in `outputs/results/headline.csv`), which is why `outputs/results/result_sentences.txt` records verbatim `S3`: `"We could not detect a benefit from adding labels from the other end of the protein."` Guess: Domain-specific feature weights and feature scaling fit on the RING start domain (`1–101`, `516` `LOF`/`FUNC` rows)—such as the large positive logistic regression weight on `from_cysteine` (`+0.7035` in `outputs/frozen.json`, where the RING domain contains the `8` PDB `1JM7` zinc-holding positions `24, 27, 39, 41, 44, 47, 61, 64` and `from_cysteine` has scaler mean `0.0872`) and the weight on `entropy` (`+0.3422`)—did not transfer to the structurally distinct BRCT end domain (`1631–1855`, where `outputs/results/by_swap.csv` shows only `9` harmful and `20` harmless `loss_of_cysteine` rows and `arm3` flagged all `20` of `20` harmless `loss_of_cysteine` swaps as false alarms), diluting the zero-shot `ESM-2` ranking signal.
